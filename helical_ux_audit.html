01 — critical issues (fix before launch)
critical
primary CTA is buried and undersized in the nav
the "fill out intake form" button in the nav is orange but sits at the same visual weight as the nav links. at 100% zoom it doesn't read as the primary action. nielsen #6 — recognition over recall: the most important action must be unmissable without hunting. reference: fly.io and railway both make their primary CTA the only colored element in the nav at 2× the text size of nav links.
fix
increase button padding, add solid background fill, ensure it's visually louder than every other nav element.
critical
the hero has two competing visual anchors
the large black headline on the left and the helix mark on the right are fighting for dominance. the eye doesn't know where to go first. on fly.io and railway the hero has one dominant element — the headline — and everything else is subordinate. the mark is too large relative to the type here.
fix
reduce mark to 60% current size. let the headline be the dominant element. mark as supporting visual, not co-headline.
critical
no single clear CTA below the hero fold
after the hero, the page has process → products → comparison → form → pricing → closing CTA. that's six sections before the next hard conversion moment. nielsen #7 — flexibility and efficiency: users who are convinced early have nowhere to convert. railway and fly.io place a secondary CTA after every 2 sections maximum.
fix
add inline CTA after the process section and after the comparison section. "start your project →" in orange text only.
critical
products section introduces scope creep in messaging
"maintenance & hosting" and "full-service development" as product categories contradict the agency positioning. you've told the user this is a fast UX delivery service — now you're also a dev shop and a hosting provider? this violates the single positioning principle and will confuse your primary target (founders who need UX work done fast). anthropic's site never wavers from its core message.
fix
cut or rename these. focus on UX deliverables only. hosting/dev can be an "ask us" item, not a featured product.
02 — medium issues (fix in v1.1)
medium
the comparison section copy is weak on the helical side
"async progress, zero fluff" is vague. the traditional agency column is specific ("2-week discovery process", "6-week delivery timeline") which makes it land. but the helical column responds with abstract benefits rather than matching specifics. asymmetric specificity makes the strong side look weaker.
fix
match specificity: "intake form, once" → "delivery in 2–5 days" → "figma files, same day". concrete vs concrete.
medium
icons in the products section break the brand system
the outlined icon style (computer, grid, code brackets, leaf) is generic UI kit energy — exactly what teenage engineering and fly.io avoid. TE uses no icons at all. railway uses simple geometric shapes or nothing. these icons signal "built in webflow template" not "considered craft".
fix
remove icons entirely. replace with numbered labels (01, 02, 03, 04) in courier new orange. the type is the design.
medium
trust bar companies include direct competitors / category overlaps
listing "stripe, vercel, linear, anthropic, figma" as clients positions you alongside the tools your clients use — not the teams that use them. your target client is a startup that uses these tools. listing the tools themselves as "trusted by" is confusing and unverifiable.
fix
replace with real clients or remove entirely until you have them. a strong "no logo wall" position is more credible than a fake one.
medium
pricing section is missing the primary differentiator
the pricing tiers list features but don't anchor on the speed value prop — which is the whole reason someone chooses helical over a traditional agency. "delivery in 2-3 days" appears briefly but isn't the lead. for a prospect comparing you to a $15k/6-week agency, the timeline is the headline in this section.
fix
add delivery timeline as the first visible spec under each tier. "2–3 days" in orange before the feature list.
medium
section spacing is inconsistent — some sections feel cramped
the process section and comparison section have noticeably different vertical rhythm. fly.io uses a strict 80px section padding rule — every section breathes identically, which makes the page feel systematic. here some sections feel rushed and others over-padded.
fix
establish a strict 80px top/bottom padding on every section. no exceptions. the grid is law.
03 — low issues (polish pass)
low
the helix mark in the hero sits inside a circle that's too light
the cream circle background is so close to the page background that the container disappears. the mark floats without a clear frame. in the icon context this works — here it just looks unfinished.
fix
either remove the circle container entirely (mark on page bg) or darken it to #E4E0D8 so it reads as a deliberate surface.
low
form preview in the intake section has a "fill in project scope" button label
this CTA label is passive. it doesn't tell the user what happens next or what they get. nielsen #4 — consistency and standards: CTAs should use action-outcome framing.
fix
"get my scope →" or "start the intake →" — active, outcome-oriented.
low
footer is undersized relative to the rest of the page
the footer copy and links are difficult to read at this scale and feel like they were added last. anthropic and railway both treat the footer as part of the brand — not an afterthought.
fix
increase footer to at least 160px height. add the "we use ai to move faster, not to think less." line. it's the most important sentence.
04 — what's working
headline copy is strong throughout
"your next ux deliverable. finished before the follow-up meeting." — this is excellent. specific, resonant, positions against a real pain point. the best headline on the page by a distance. keep it.
color system is disciplined
orange accent appears rarely and always with purpose. the ink/paper palette is consistent. no rogue blues or purples. this is better restraint than most first-pass designs show.
the comparison section structure is right
dark background, two-column before/after is the correct approach. the visual logic is sound — traditional agency = old/bad, helical = new/good. just needs sharper copy on the right side.
intake form preview is a smart conversion element
showing the form fields inline as a preview is a genuinely good UX decision. it demystifies the process and reduces friction before clicking. this is differentiated — most agencies don't do this.
typography scale feels right at a page level
the hierarchy between headline sizes, subheads, and body is well-calibrated. the heavy-weight headline against lighter body weight creates the right visual rhythm.
the closing CTA section is clean
"fill out the form. get a scope by tomorrow." — concrete promise, specific timeline. the only suggestion is making the orange button fill solid rather than outline at this stage of the page — the user needs a clear final action.
05 — benchmark gaps (vs fly.io · railway · teenage engineering)
fly.io
terminal density + code presence
fly.io's hero has a terminal block that's the dominant right-side element. it establishes "this is for builders" immediately. helical's helix mark is beautiful but abstract — it doesn't signal "we work with technical founders".
gap — add terminal or code element to hero
railway
section numbering discipline
railway numbers every section (01, 02, 03) systematically, creating a sense that the page was designed as a system, not assembled. helical does this inconsistently — some sections have numbers, some don't.
gap — number every section consistently
teenage engineering
no icons, type does all the work
TE has zero decorative icons. every visual element is either a product photo or pure typography. helical's UI-kit icons in the products section are the single biggest aesthetic departure from the TE/fly.io reference direction.
gap — remove icons, use numbered labels
anthropic
whitespace as confidence signal
anthropic's sections are spacious to the point of feeling academic. that spaciousness communicates "we don't need to cram in more information." helical's middle sections feel slightly cramped by comparison — the process and products sections especially.
gap — increase section padding 20–30%
replit
single clear primary action
replit's entire page points to one action. every CTA says the same thing in slightly different words. helical has "fill out intake form", "see how it works", "get started", "start here", "talk to us" — too many different labels for what is essentially one conversion path.
gap — unify CTA language to one phrase
all five
monospace as brand material
all five reference sites use monospace type as a design element — not just for code. helical has this in the right places but the section labels and eyebrows inconsistently switch between sans and mono.
close — audit and standardise mono usage
